# nonprofit-it-compliance-checklist
A free, plain-language checklist to help small nonprofits meet funder and compliance requirements for IT on a tight budget.
Nonprofit IT Compliance Checklist

Nobody starts a nonprofit because they love IT policy. But funders, insurers, and donors increasingly ask for proof of responsible technology practices, and most small teams handle IT with a tight budget and a part-time “IT person” who is really the office manager. This repository gives you a clear order of operations and a way to keep proof of your work.

Who this is for
Small and mid-sized nonprofits with little or no dedicated IT staff
Office managers, program leads, and volunteers who “also do IT”
Anyone preparing for a grant report, audit, or insurance renewal
How to use this checklist
Click Use this template or fork the repository.
Work through the sections below in order.
Tick items off as you complete them and note the date.
Keep the finished checklist in your compliance folder as evidence for funders and auditors.
Checklist
1. Know what you’re required to do
 List the types of data we hold (donor payments, health details, children’s records, and so on)
 Identify which rules may apply to us, such as GDPR, US state privacy laws, or India’s data protection rules and FCRA for foreign contributions
 Read the technology and data clauses in every current grant agreement
 Confirm our payment processor’s requirements if we accept card payments
 Summarize all of the above on one page
2. Build an inventory
 List every system we use (donor database, email, accounting, website, shared drives)
 Note what data each system holds
 Note who has access to each system
 Remove old accounts, former volunteers, and forgotten personal cloud storage
3. Low-cost security controls
 Turn on multi-factor authentication (MFA) for email, donor database, banking, and cloud storage
 Adopt a team password manager and retire shared password spreadsheets
 Enable automatic updates on all devices, browsers, and apps
 Back up critical data (three copies, two storage types, one offsite)
 Test a restore from backup at least once
 Limit access to what each role needs
 Encrypt laptops and phones
4. Claim nonprofit discounts
 Check eligibility for TechSoup or similar regional programs
 Check Google for Nonprofits and Microsoft’s nonprofit offers
 Ask cloud and security vendors about nonprofit credits or pricing

Offers and eligibility change, so confirm current terms with each provider.

5. Write short policies

Aim for one or two pages each.

 Acceptable use of devices and accounts
 Data handling (what we collect, why, how long we keep it, who sees it)
 Incident response (who to call and what to do)
 Onboarding and offboarding (how access is granted and removed)
6. Train your team
 Run a 30-minute security awareness session once or twice a year
 Show real phishing examples
 Make it clear that reporting a mistake quickly is welcome
 Record the date, topics, and attendees
7. Keep records
 Store the inventory, policies, training records, access reviews, and backup test results in one folder
 Update the folder after every review
8. Know when to get help

Consider a part-time consultant or managed IT provider if:

 We handle sensitive data (health, immigration, financial, or children’s records)
 A funder requires a formal assessment or certification
 We are migrating systems
 We have outgrown volunteer-only IT support
9. Keep a rhythm
 Quarterly: review access and test backups (about 30 minutes)
 Annually: review policies
 Annually: refresh staff training
Suggested review schedule
Frequency	Task
Quarterly	Access review, backup test
Twice a year	Permissions review
Annually	Policy review, staff training refresher
On every departure	Remove accounts the same day
